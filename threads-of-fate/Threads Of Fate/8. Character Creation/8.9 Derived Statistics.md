Let's get a deeper explanation and understanding for what the attributes actually do for us, here we will explain game mechanics that do not appear elsewhere in this book. These derived statistics can shape a character into something unique. They are passive elements that can determine how you play.
 
As well as your attributes and skills, you also have a number of derived statistics. These derived statistics are passive elements of your character, telling you how much weight you can carry, how difficult you are for an opponent to hit, how quickly you act in combat, and how resistant you are to being hurt.
 
**Carry Weight**  
Carry weight is a stat that determines how much you character is able to carry and when it becomes cumbersome. Your carry weight is 70kg + 5 x Strength. If you are carrying more than you carry weight you become over-encumbered reducing you Agility by one, then for every 10kg over you carry weight you lose an additional point in Agility. If this would reduce you below 1 Agility, then you can no longer move until you have removed the excess weight.
 
**Inspiration Pool**  
Inspiration Pool is the amount of IP you can save between rounds.
 
**Social Modifier**  
Social Modifier is a special stat that affects any skill test where your charm(or lack of) could influence the results, simply add this when calculating your TN. If you are in doubt ask your GM if this bonus applies. Can only be applied to Roleplaying skills.
 
**Area of Effect**  
Increases or decreases the Area of Effect of spells and abilities. The number listed is the radius counted by hexes or length counted by hexes. If this would reduce the size below 1, then reduce the damage dice one level for each reduction below 1.
 
**Bestiary Progression**  
This is a stat that determines how much you learn about a creature for each one you kill. Bestiary is further described in the Exploration section of this book.
