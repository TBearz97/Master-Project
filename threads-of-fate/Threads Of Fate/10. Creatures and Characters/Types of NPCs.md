A non-player character—normally abbreviated to NPC—is any character controlled by you, the gamemaster. You decide on an NPC’s motives, their nature, and the actions they take. They have a full set of attributes and derived statistics.
 
LEVEL  
Like player characters, all NPCs have a level. This is a rough indicator of how hardy and dangerous a creature or character is overall, and how much of a challenge they are to overcome.
 
TYPE  
All NPCs fall into one of several categories:

- Normal NPCs are the default, following the normal rules as laid out in this chapter. - Mighty creatures are more potent versions of a common type of creature, which are more dangerous than their level alone would indicate. They’re somewhat rare, and they tend to be encountered as leaders if that type of creature is found in groups. - Legendary creatures are strange, mutated versions of common creatures, which adapt quickly when hurt. They’re exceedingly rare and tend to be much more potent than other creatures of their level.
    
KEYWORDS  
NPCs often have an additional keyword or two that notes something specific about them. This has no direct effect on the rules, but other rules may refer to these keywords, and they can serve as a useful reminder of an NPC’s underlying nature or allegiance. Common keywords include:

- Beast
- Dragon
- Eldritch
- Elemental
- Kith
- Undead
- Virtuous (Angles)
- Vicious (Demons)
 
NPC REACTIONS  
When you encounter an NPC, they relate to you in one of three ways:

- Allies are friendly and try to help the PCs, even in dangerous situations if they think they can be of assistance. When allied NPCs generate extra IP in combat, it gets added to the group pool. Allies may seek out the PCs to ask them for help.
- Enemies are hostile and seek to hinder or hurt you. This may normally result in a fight, but some foes might try to cause you problems in other ways.
- Bystanders don’t really know or care about you one way or another. They may be willing to trade with the PCs or help out in some (non-violent) ways if there’s something in it for them, but they won’t go out of their way to help or hinder the PCs otherwise. They may also approach the PCs asking for help, offering a reward in exchange; this is often a way to turn bystanders into allies.
 
CREATURES  
A creature has a small number of abilities used to define their skill tests, attacks, and other functions.

- Level and Type: A listing of the creature’s level, and its Type. This will also list the amount of XP the creature is worth when defeated or overcome.
    
    - A Minion creature does not gain anything from leveling up.
- Keywords: This is a listing of any keywords that describe the creature.
- Attributes: Characters have the full range of attributes, with each attribute rated from 1-10.
    
    - A Minion creature's attributes added together total 38.
    - A Normal creature’s attributes added together total 40.
    - A Mighty creature’s attributes added together total 42.
    - A Legendary creature’s attributes added together total 42. Legendary creatures also have 1 selected attribute which is Legendary, giving them a unique bonus explained below.
- Skills: Creatures NPCs have skills exactly as player characters do, with each skill rated from 0 to 6.
    
    - A Minion creature has 2 Tag Skill. Then, they receive a number of extra skill points equal to 9 plus their INT.
    - A Normal creature has 3 Tag Skills. Then, they receive a number of extra skill points equal to 9 plus their INT plus their level.
    - A Mighty character has 4 Tag Skills. Then, they receive a number of extra skill points equal to their INT plus their level.
    - A Legendary character has 4 Tag Skills. Then, they receive a number of extra skill points equal to their INT plus their level.
- Defense: The difficulty of attacks made against the creature. This will normally be 1, but might be changed further by perks or other effects.
- Initiative: A creature’s Initiative score. It is equal to the creature’s PER plus their AGI score.
- Health points: A Minion creature’s health points are equal to their END plus their RES score.
    
    - A Normal creature has additional HP equal to their Level.
    - A Mighty creature has additional HP equal to their Level and END score.
    - A Legendary creature has additional HP equal to their Level and END score.
- Damage Resistances: Just as with a player character, a creature’s damage resistances reduce damage suffered before decreasing health points.
- Attacks: The methods the creature can use to attack in combat. Each attack lists the test’s target number (TN), damage, damage type, damage effects, and any qualities that apply to its weapons.
    
    - Attacks using ranged weapons from creatures do not require tracking ammunition.
- Special Abilities: Any special abilities the creature possesses, or unique actions the creature can take.
- Perks: Like the player characters, a creature should have a perk for every other Level they have. Some creatures have access to special Perks based on their Keywords.
- Inventory: A listing of the equipment and other items the creature is carrying.

_Grouped NPCs_  
_NPCs can act in groups if you find it easier to manage at_ _your table. A group consists of 2-5 identical creatures or_ _characters, who take a single combined turn during a conflict_ _or otherwise attempt a skill test together._ _When a group attempts a test, a single NPC within the group_ _attempts the test, and each other NPC in the group assists that_ _leader. As the dice gained as part of acting as a_ _group are a form of assistance, they do not count towards the_ _normal limits on buying bonus d20s with Action Points._

- _Making an Attack: When you attack with a group,_ _add +1 CD to your damage roll for each NPC in the_ _group beyond the first._
- _Being Targeted: When they are attacked, NPCs_ _within the group are targeted and take damage_ _and injuries individually._
- _Defeat: When a creature in the group has been reduced_ _to 0 health points, they are defeated, but any remaining_ _damage from the attack is lost—extra damage isn’t_ _carried over to other NPCs in the group._

_Followers and Companions_  
_If you have allied NPCs alongside the player characters, the players can command_ _them in combat. NPCs under a PC’s command have only a_ _limited range of actions they can take, and they follow a few_ _basic rules._

- _An allied NPC does not use their own Initiative score;_ _rather, they act immediately before or after the PC that_ _controls them._
- _They automatically perform any minor actions needed to_ _keep up with the player character that commands them,_ _or to follow an order issued. Inspiration Points do not need to_ _be spent for the NPC to perform these minor actions._
- _An NPC may not take any major action other than Assist_ _without being commanded to do so. Commanding an_ _NPC to take a major action is itself a major action._
