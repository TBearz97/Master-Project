The rules in Threads of Fate are there to support tension, clarity, and meaningful choices. As the GM, your job is not to apply every rule at all times. Your job is to decide when a rule adds something useful to the moment and when a ruling will serve the table better.

###### **Call for Rolls with Purpose**

Ask for a roll when the outcome is uncertain, when failure would matter, or when the method a player chooses meaningfully changes the situation. If the action is trivial, impossible, or uninteresting to resolve, simply decide the outcome and move on.

###### **Respect the Fiction First**

Players describe what they are trying to do before skills are chosen. Let the situation guide what is possible. If a skill does not fit the fiction, do not offer it. If a plan is especially clever, well-prepared, or strongly supported by the scene, lower the pressure instead of forcing a difficult roll.

###### **Be Consistent, Not Rigid**

Consistency builds trust. If you rule that a certain approach usually uses a certain skill, try to keep that logic stable from scene to scene. At the same time, stay open to unusual ideas when the player gives a believable explanation.

###### **Protect the Pace**

Do not let rules slow the game down more than the moment deserves. Small details can be resolved with a quick ruling. Save longer discussions for breaks, after the session, or for moments where the decision will shape future play.

###### **Let Failure Change the Situation**

Failure should usually move the story somewhere rather than stop it entirely. A failed test might cost time, create danger, reveal the characters, worsen the terms of success, attract attention, or force a harder choice. Try not to let important progress depend on a single pass-or-stop roll.

###### **Use Transparency Well**

Players make better choices when they understand the stakes. Tell them what the risk is when that knowledge would be clear to their characters. Hidden information is useful when uncertainty is the point, but secrecy should create tension, not confusion.

###### **Adjust for Your Table**

Some groups want careful simulation and resource pressure. Others want momentum, drama, and quick rulings. Threads of Fate can support either style. If a rule is consistently making your game less fun, change it openly and apply the new approach consistently.
