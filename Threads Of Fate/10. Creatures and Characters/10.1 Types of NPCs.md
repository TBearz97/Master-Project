A non-player character, or NPC, is any creature or person controlled by the GM. NPCs use the same broad rules language as player characters, but they are built to run quickly at the table and to serve the needs of the scene.

###### **Level**

Every NPC has a level. Level is a rough measure of danger, resilience, and overall capability. A creature's level should reflect how challenging it is to fight, survive, outwit, or otherwise overcome.

###### **Type**

NPCs fall into four broad types.

- **Minion:** A weak or disposable foe. Minions are useful for crowds, packs, guards, and lesser threats.
- **Normal:** The standard benchmark for most creatures and characters.
- **Mighty:** A stronger version of a creature, champion, elite guard, leader, or dangerous specialist.
- **Legendary:** A rare and exceptional threat whose abilities go beyond ordinary limits.

Use type to decide how much attention and mechanical depth the NPC deserves. A Minion can be simple. A Legendary creature should feel distinct and memorable.

###### **Keywords**

Keywords describe what an NPC is. They do not do anything by themselves unless another rule refers to them, but they are important for magic, perks, resistances, vulnerabilities, and narrative logic.

Common keywords include:

- Beast
- Dragon
- Eldritch
- Elemental
- Kith
- Undead
- Virtuous
- Vicious

Add faction, profession, or role keywords when useful, such as `Bandit`, `Knight`, `Cultist`, `Scout`, or `Riverfolk`.

###### **Reactions**

When the party encounters an NPC, that NPC usually begins in one of three broad attitudes:

- **Ally:** Friendly and inclined to help.
- **Enemy:** Hostile and willing to hinder, threaten, or attack.
- **Bystander:** Not committed either way, but capable of becoming an ally, problem, witness, or future contact.

An NPC's reaction is not fixed forever. Fear, reward, shame, loyalty, magic, and changing circumstances can all shift an NPC from one category to another.

###### **What an NPC Entry Should Include**

At minimum, a useful NPC entry should include:

- name or role
- level and type
- keywords
- attributes
- relevant skills
- defenses
- health and vitality
- attacks or notable actions
- special traits, abilities, or perks
- equipment or rewards if those matter

Not every NPC needs equal detail. A peasant witness may only need a few skills and a clear motive. A boss creature should have a full combat profile.

###### **Attributes and Skills**

NPCs use the current six attributes:

- Might
- Dexterity
- Constitution
- Perception
- Intelligence
- Resolve

NPCs also use the current skill list. Most NPCs do not need every skill written down. Record the skills that matter for how they are expected to act in play.

If a skill is unlisted, assume the NPC is untrained or average in that area.

###### **Defenses**

NPCs use the same four defenses as the rest of the game:

- **Deflection** against direct attacks
- **Reflex** against area attacks and hazards
- **Endurance** against bodily harm, force, poison, and disease
- **Mind** against mental pressure and magical influence

You may derive these directly from attributes, or set them by feel if you are building a fast improvised NPC.

###### **Health, Vitality, and Concentration**

NPCs should use Health, Vitality, and Concentration in the same way as player characters.

- **Health** determines how long they stay conscious and combat-effective.
- **Vitality** determines how close they are to death.
- **Concentration** determines how well they hold together under pressure, especially when using queued actions, magic, or demanding abilities.

Ordinary NPCs can use simplified tracking when needed. If a creature is not important enough to deserve detailed injury play, it is fine to defeat it once the scene has clearly turned against it.

###### **Attacks and Abilities**

NPCs should attack using the same combat framework as everyone else: roll `d20 + Accuracy`, compare against the relevant defense, then apply damage, DT, damage types, and effects.

Not every NPC needs a huge action list. Most work well with:

- one or two reliable attacks
- one signature trick, spell, or combat action
- one reactive or passive trait if they are important

Mighty and Legendary NPCs should have something that changes how the scene feels, not just bigger numbers.
