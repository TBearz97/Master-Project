NPCs can be removed from a scene in many ways. They may be killed, driven off, captured, surrendered, tricked, routed, or otherwise overcome. The method matters to the fiction, but all of these can count as resolving the threat if the danger has been meaningfully dealt with.

###### **Defeated and Overcome**

- **Defeated** usually means the NPC has been physically beaten, disabled, slain, or rendered incapable of continuing.
- **Overcome** means the NPC's threat or opposition has been dealt with without necessarily killing them.

Examples of overcoming an NPC include:

- sneaking past a guarded position and escaping cleanly
- convincing the enemy not to fight
- trapping or restraining them
- forcing a retreat
- exposing them so they lose authority or support

Merely delaying an NPC is not enough if that threat returns immediately in the same scene or objective.

###### **What Happens at 0 Health**

For most ordinary enemies, reaching 0 Health means they are out of the fight. Whether they die, fall unconscious, flee, or surrender depends on the fiction and on the intent of the attacker.

Useful defaults:

- unimportant hostile NPCs usually die or are permanently disabled unless the party says otherwise
- important NPCs may fall unconscious, begin dying, surrender, or remain available for capture
- beasts often flee if badly hurt, unless cornered, maddened, or defending territory or young
- intelligent foes rarely fight to the death without a reason

Mighty and Legendary NPCs can be given more dramatic endings if that better suits the scene.

###### **XP Awards**

When an enemy NPC is defeated or overcome, each PC receives XP based on the NPC's level and type.

- **Minion** creatures are always worth 8 XP.
- **Mighty** creatures are worth double the listed XP for their level.
- **Legendary** creatures are worth triple the listed XP for their level.

###### **When Not to Award XP**

Do not award XP for a threat that was only temporarily bypassed and remains functionally unresolved.

Examples:

- sneaking past a guard who immediately raises the alarm afterward
- hiding from a monster for a few minutes without escaping its territory
- delaying an enemy patrol that simply rejoins the fight moments later

If the danger is still fully present, it has not been overcome.

###### **Loot and Rewards**

When an NPC is defeated, the party may take whatever gear, valuables, and supplies logically remain.

Common reward types include:

- weapons and armor
- potions and consumables
- gold or valuables
- documents, keys, maps, and clues
- tools or materials
- mounts, transport, or access

Not every enemy should be carrying a full reward package. Give loot that makes sense for the NPC's role and circumstances.

###### **Other Rewards**

In addition to gear, you may award:

- **Butchery:** beasts and monsters may provide meat, hide, bone, venom, or magical components
- **Gold:** intelligent NPCs may carry coin or trade valuables suitable to their station
- **Materials:** scavengers, crafters, and monsters may leave behind useful crafting materials
- **Information:** letters, symbols, orders, rumors, or witnesses can matter more than equipment

For quick material rewards, choose one or two fitting materials for an ordinary NPC, and two to four for a notable scavenger, elite, or unusual creature.

###### **Mercy, Capture, and Consequences**

Defeating an NPC without killing them can have lasting consequences. Prisoners can be questioned, spared enemies can return, witnesses can spread rumors, and survivors can become allies, recurring villains, or sources of future trouble.

Let those choices matter. If the party spares, captures, or humiliates an enemy, the world should remember it.
