When building a creature or character for the GM side of the game, start with the role it plays in the story and the scene. The numbers exist to support that role, not the other way around.

###### **Step 1: Choose the Role**

Decide what the NPC is supposed to do.

Examples:

- front-line bruiser
- evasive skirmisher
- ranged hunter
- spellcaster
- beast ambusher
- social manipulator
- commander
- support healer

If you know the role, it becomes much easier to choose the right attributes, attacks, skills, and special traits.

###### **Step 2: Choose Level and Type**

Choose the NPC's level, then choose whether it is a Minion, Normal, Mighty, or Legendary creature.

- **Minion:** weak but useful in numbers
- **Normal:** the standard baseline
- **Mighty:** stronger than a normal creature of the same level
- **Legendary:** a rare centerpiece enemy or exceptional ally

Type should change how much spotlight the NPC can survive, not just its raw numbers.

###### **Step 3: Assign Attributes**

If you want to build from a quick budget, use these totals:

- **Minion:** 38 attribute points
- **Normal:** 40 attribute points
- **Mighty:** 42 attribute points
- **Legendary:** 42 attribute points, plus one Legendary attribute

Use the current six attributes only:

- Might
- Dexterity
- Constitution
- Perception
- Intelligence
- Resolve

These totals are guidelines, not prison bars. Shift a point or two when it helps the concept.

###### **Step 4: Assign Skills**

Do not try to build every NPC like a player character. Instead, assign only the skills that matter.

As a quick guideline:

- **Minion:** 2 to 4 relevant skills
- **Normal:** 4 to 6 relevant skills
- **Mighty:** 5 to 7 relevant skills
- **Legendary:** 6 to 8 relevant skills

For skill ratings, this is a useful default:

- **0 to 2:** poor, casual, or incidental training
- **3 to 4:** competent and reliable
- **5 to 6:** strong specialist
- **7 to 8:** exceptional expert
- **9 to 10:** legendary master

Most NPCs should only have one or two truly high skills.

###### **Step 5: Derive Combat Stats**

Build the NPC's combat values from the same logic used by player characters.

- **Action Points** come primarily from Dexterity.
- **Accuracy** comes primarily from Perception.
- **Deflection** comes primarily from Dexterity and equip load.
- **Reflex** comes from Dexterity and Perception.
- **Endurance** is supported by Might and Constitution.
- **Mind** is supported by Intelligence and Resolve.
- **Health** and **Vitality** are primarily driven by Constitution and level.
- **Concentration** is primarily driven by Resolve.

If you need a fast build, it is fine to estimate rather than calculate every number perfectly. The goal is to create a creature that behaves credibly within the current rules.

###### **Step 6: Choose Equipment, Natural Protection, or Both**

An NPC can gain Damage Threshold from armor, from natural hide, from magic, or from a combination of these.

Use the equipment chapter when the creature is wearing real gear. Use natural protection when a beast, monster, or supernatural entity is naturally hard to injure. In either case, decide how the NPC resists:

- physical damage
- magical damage
- necrotic damage

Heavier protection should usually come with lower mobility, lower stealth, or a weaker Deflection profile.

###### **Step 7: Give It Attacks**

Most NPCs only need one to three attacks or combat actions.

Good attack design principles:

- one dependable default action
- one situational or tactical option
- one signature option for important enemies

Natural attacks such as claws, bites, horns, tail strikes, or crushing limbs should use the same damage, effects, and defense logic as weapon attacks. If a creature's body is its weapon, write the attack as clearly as you would for a sword or spell.

###### **Step 8: Add Traits, Perks, or Special Rules**

Traits should make the NPC play differently, not just hit harder.

Useful trait categories include:

- mobility traits
- hunting or ambush traits
- resistances or vulnerabilities
- spellcasting access
- command or morale effects
- regeneration or persistence
- unusual hit locations or body structure

As a rule of thumb:

- **Minion:** 0 to 1 trait
- **Normal:** 1 to 2 traits
- **Mighty:** 2 to 4 traits
- **Legendary:** 3 or more major traits

If a trait only adds bookkeeping and no drama, remove it.

###### **Legendary Attributes**

Legendary creatures should have one attribute pushed beyond ordinary limits. A Legendary attribute should create a real identity for the creature.

Examples:

- **Legendary Might:** greater damage, terrifying physical force, or impossible carrying power
- **Legendary Dexterity:** superior AP economy, evasiveness, or impossible speed
- **Legendary Constitution:** unusual resilience, regeneration, or refusal to fall
- **Legendary Perception:** exceptional Accuracy, uncanny awareness, or deadly critical pressure
- **Legendary Intelligence:** superior Potency, planning, or tactical adaptation
- **Legendary Resolve:** exceptional Concentration, mental resistance, or supernatural presence

Do not give a Legendary attribute unless you want the creature to feel extraordinary in play.

###### **Building Beasts**

Beasts are often best built from a simple base profile plus one or two defining traits.

Common beast traits include:

- pack hunting
- pounce or charge
- venom
- flight
- burrowing
- thick hide
- camouflage
- trampling size

A beast should feel like an animal first. Its rules should express how it moves, hunts, flees, or corners prey.

###### **Building Kith and Other Intelligent NPCs**

Humanoid and other intelligent NPCs are usually defined by role, equipment, and social context.

Important questions:

- what is their job?
- what gear do they actually carry?
- what are they willing to die for?
- what do they do when they start losing?

Many intelligent enemies should retreat, bargain, surrender, call for help, or change tactics instead of fighting to the death.

###### **Building Casters**

Spellcasting NPCs do not need a full player-style spell list. Give them the handful of spells and magical actions that matter for the scene.

Most caster NPCs work well with:

- one reliable attack spell
- one control or utility spell
- one defensive, mobility, or narrative spell

Legendary casters may deserve more, but ordinary hostile casters should stay compact enough to run quickly.

###### **Final Check**

Before using an NPC, ask:

- does it have a clear role?
- does it have at least one interesting choice in combat?
- does it fit the current attribute and defense system?
- can I run it quickly at the table?

If the answer to the last question is no, simplify it.
