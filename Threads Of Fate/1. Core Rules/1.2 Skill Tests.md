When you want your character to attempt an action, you'll perform a skill test. This involves rolling one twenty-sided dice (d20) and adding your character's skill to determine the result.
 
# **Dice Result Table**

| **Total** | **Result**                               |
| :-------: | :--------------------------------------- |
|    20+    | Hit/Success + 2 IP, +1 IP per 5 above 20 |
|  16 - 20  | Hit/Success + 1 IP                       |
|  11 - 15  | Hit/Success                              |
|  6 - 10   | Fail / Graze (Combat Only)               |
|   1 - 5   | Fail                                     |
|  -4 - 0   | Complication                             |
|  -9 - -5  | Two Complications                        |

# **Skill Rank**  
Skill Rank determines how good you are at a chosen skill. Meaning when you attempt a skill test, you can add your Rank to the value of your rolled dice to improve your odds.
 
# **Difficulty**  
Difficulties can either be a number chosen by the GM or tied to an opponents skills, these are typically between 0 and 10. When making a skill test you subtract any difficulties from your total. For example, I have a Athletics Rank of 2 and roll a 10 on an associated test, but the test has a Difficulty 5. That means that my total is 10 + 2 - 5, resulting in a 7.
 
# **Natural 20s and Natural 1s**  
When rolling a d20, if the value of the dice lands on 20 or 1, then these are called Natural 20 and Natural 1 respectively. When you roll a Natural 20 you gain a bonus of +5 to your roll, while rolling a Natural 1 gives you a penalty of -5.
 
# **Assistance**  
Difficult tasks are better with teamwork, you can assist someone who is attempting a test if your GM allows it. When you do you explain how you are assisting, this does not need to be with the same skill, then you add half of your chosen skill to their skill test.
 
# **Struggle**  
In some situations, the difficulty of a task is not determined by the task itself, but by a force trying to oppose your success. This is called a Struggle. During a Struggle, you test gains a Difficulty based on the opposing player (usually the GM) applicable skill.
 
# **Group Tests**  
Certain situation require the whole effort of the group in order to succeed. When this occurs the party must preform a group test. Every one in the party makes a skill test with a related skill for the scenario, then you count up how many succeeded. Normal success counts as one, but you can buy another if you score 2 IP in the process. Complications count as a negative. The goal is to score enough successes equal to half the party (rounded up). Should the party fail, it will automatically result in a complication.
 
# **Complications in Skill Tests** 
During a skill test, if you manage to roll a total that is below one you create a complication. Complications introduces a new problem to the situation. Examples of complications could be, you snap a bowstring, your sword gets stuck in a nearby tree, while sneaking you tripped which alerted the guards, or other similar problems. Certain situation might not have any complications to occur or your GM might not think of one on the spot (they have enough to think about), in these situations the GM can choose to instead just generate 2 IP for themselves.
 
# **Voluntary Failure** 
Some situations might just be to difficult and not worth the risk of creating further complications. When this occurs you may ask your GM for a voluntary failure, if they grant it you will receive one complication, one Luck Point and you cannot try another skill test at the same situation.
 
# **Success at a Cost**  
In certain situation a success might be too important to fail. In these situations the GM might agree that you can succeed at a cost. This could either be by introducing complications, paying a high IP cost, generating IP for the GM, or something else. When you decide that you are willing to succeed at a cost, if the GM agrees you can, then the GM must present you with what the cost is before you make the final decision.
