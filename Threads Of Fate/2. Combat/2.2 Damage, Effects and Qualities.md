# **Damage Dice**  
When your attack hits its target, you inflict an amount of damage determined by the weapon’s damage dice. 
 
# **Critical Damage**  
Critical Damage occurs when you roll max on your damage dice, when this happens you also get to roll your weapons Crit Dice. For example you strike with a dagger that has a D4 as Damage Dice, you roll a 4. That means for a dagger you also get to roll a D8 and add that damage to the hit. If you are asked to temporarily decrease the level of your Damage Dice then you cannot crit while the level of your Damage Dice is decreased.
 
# **Damage Origin**  
There are three damage origins in the roleplaying game: physical, magical and necrotic.
- Physical: Anything damage from the physical world and has not been affected by magic.
- Magical: Anything made with magical origin or affected by magic.
- Necrotic: Exposure to Undead, or dark magic.
Each target has a Damage Threshold (DT) for each of these types, based on their clothing, armor, or naturally tough hides and chitin.
 
# **Damage Types**  
Damage types are specified types of damage that exist regardless of origin. These types are used to describe Resistances, Vulnerabilities, Bonuses or Penalties. For example if a character has Resistance to Fire, then that character will take half damage from anything that is considered Fire, regardless if that Fire is from a Bonfire, a Fire spell or Necrotic Fire. # **Damage Thresholds**  
Damage Thresholds are values usually found on armor that reduce the total damage taken from an attack. These number reduce the incoming damage by a flat amount. For example, if a character has a physical damage threshold of 3, and he takes 7 damage from an attack, the total damage taken would only be 4 damage because the armor reduced it by 3.
 
# **Resistances and Vulnerabilities**  
On top of damage thresholds and damage types, your character as well as NPC's can obtain Resistances and Vulnerabilities. These are more specific, like fire resistance or frost vulnerability. Meaning that for resistances, you take half damage against any attack that fits description and for vulnerabilities you take double damage from any attack that fits the description. Resistances and Vulnerabilities are applied after damage threshold. For example, fire resistance halves your damage regardless if the fire is magical or physical.
 
# **Defenses**  
Defenses are passive values that protect you from attacks and effects. Accuracy rolls are compared against defenses to determine whether attacks hit, and Potency rolls are compared against defenses to determine whether effects are applied.

- Deflection: Represents a character's ability to deflect direct melee and ranged attacks.
- Reflex: Represents a character's ability to dodge area of effect attacks.
- Endurance: Represents a character's resistance to bodily harm, forceful effects, poison, and disease.
- Mind: Represents a character's strength of mind and resistance to mental attack.

# **Concentration**  
Concentration represents how well you hold together your planned actions under pressure during combat. Each time you take damage, your Concentration is reduced. Particularly heavy hits reduce it further. If your Concentration reaches 0 before your queued actions are resolved, those actions are interrupted. If your actions have already been completed, then instead you suffer a Speed penalty on your next round.

# **Trauma**
Trauma is a particularly severe amount of damage dealt to a single location. When damage to a location is high enough to count as Trauma, the target suffers an Injury at that location and reduces Concentration by 2. The exact threshold for causing Trauma is explained with injuries and recovery.
 
# **Range**  
Melee weapons are only effective against targets in an adjacent hex, but ranged and thrown weapons can be used to attack any target you can see, but they vary in effectiveness depending on how far away the target is from you. Ranged weapons have an ideal range noted in their profile (just listed as Range), which is based on the relative distance between the attacker and the target, using the zones of the combat environment. A weapon’s ideal range is one of the following:

- Adjacent (A): The weapon is most effective against targets in an adjacent hex.
- Close (C): The weapon is most effective against targets within 2-4 hexes.
- Medium (M): The weapon is most effective against targets within 5-7 hexes.
- Long (L): The weapon is most effective against targets within 8-10.
- Extreme (X): The weapon is most effective against targets 11-13.
- Attacking beyond a weapon's maximum effective range is still possible, but suffers an Accuracy penalty.

A ranged attack made while threatened in melee suffers an Accuracy penalty. Attacking from a weapon's ideal range grants a bonus, and may also grant a damage bonus depending on the attack or weapon being used.

# **Conditions**
- **Burning:**
	A burning creature suffers ongoing fire damage until the flames are extinguished. Water, smothering the flames, or a suitable Clear action can end this condition.

- **Poisoned:**
	A poisoned creature is suffering from a toxin. The exact penalties and duration depend on the poison that caused the condition.

- **Prone:**
	You may be knocked prone by attacks, hazards, or complications. You may also drop prone willingly, at the end of any movement action you perform. Being prone has the following effects:
	- When you’re prone, you crawl. Your movement is slowed.
	- While you’re prone, enemies at range suffer an Accuracy penalty when attacking you.
	- While you’re prone, enemies adjacent to you gain an Accuracy bonus when attacking you.
