# **Health and Vitality**  
A characters health and vitality is determined by their Endurance attribute. Health helps determine how much damage a character can take in combat while Vitality determines how much damage a character can take before they die. When a character takes damage you lower both your current Health and Vitality, however when you receive healing of any kind only Health can be restored and only up to your Health maximum. Vitality can only be restored by resting. Should a characters Health be reduced to 0, then the character will be knocked unconscious and suffer 1 injury at the location struck. Should a characters Vitality be reduced to 0, then that character is dead.

# **Trauma**
If you take enough damage to a location, it is considered Trauma. Trauma represents a particularly severe blow that overwhelms the target at that location. When a character suffers Trauma, they take an Injury at that location and reduce their Concentration by 2.

# **Injuries**  
An injury occurs when a character suffers Trauma. An injury on the character confers a penalty depending on the location hit.
- **Arm:** 
	You drop any object held in that hand, and the arm is broken or otherwise unable to move. You cannot perform any actions using that arm—by itself or alongside your other arm.
- **Leg:** 
	You immediately fall prone as your leg gives out under your weight. If one leg is broken, your movement is hindered. If both are broken you remain prone until healed.
- **Torso:** 
	You suffer Internal Bleeding, a more severe form of bleeding that continues to harm you over time until it is treated or stops naturally.
- **Head:** 
	You are stunned for one turn. After that, your Perception is reduced until the injury has received medical attention.
 
# **Dying**  
When your character is reduced to 0 Health, they suffer an injury to the location struck, and then fall unconscious. If their Vitality is reduced to 0, they are dead. Any character with 3 or more injuries will remain unconscious until healed. Should the entire party fall unconscious but not die out right, then the GM should set up a new scenario based on how they were defeated.
 
# **Healing**  
When you’ve taken damage, there are a number of ways you can recover. In the midst of combat, healing potions, magic and first aid are the only ways to heal, but outside of combat you have several options. The Medicine and Resistance skills are the most useful skills for healing.
 
# **Stabilizing The Unconscious**  
Using the First Aid action, you can attempt to stabilize an unconscious character. This requires a Medicine test, where more severe injuries make the task harder. Passing the test restores the character to 1 Health and they are no longer unconscious. If necrotic damage has reduced their maximum Health to 0, they are in a coma and cannot be stabilized until their maximum Health is above 0 again by healing the necrotic damage.
 
# **Treating an Injury**  
You can attempt a Medicine test to treat an injury. Passing the test allows a patient to ignore the penalties of their injury. An injury treated using First Aid is not fully healed: it has merely been patched up so that it no longer imposes a penalty. If a treated location suffers damage again, that injury may reopen. Completely recovering from an injury takes time.
 
# **Rest**  
When you sleep, you regain your maximum Health, and injuries that have already been treated may fully heal. Untreated injuries heal more slowly and may require successful recovery tests or additional care. In order to restore Vitality you need to either eat food or go a long time without suffering damage. Eating food can restore both Health and Vitality, but only a little at a time. If you stay safe and avoid harm for long enough, your Vitality is restored to full.
 
# **Medical Attention**  
Characters may require long-term medical care if they’ve been injured, poisoned, or are suffering from a disease. A single character can provide medical attention for several patients at once depending on their skill. For injuries, each day of rest and medical attention improves the patient's chances of recovery. For poisons and diseases, proper care can also help the patient recover more quickly. Full rest makes this process easier.
