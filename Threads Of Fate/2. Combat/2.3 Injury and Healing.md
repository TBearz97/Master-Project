# **Health and Vitality**  
A characters health and vitality is determined by their Endurance attribute. Health helps determine how much damage a character can take in combat while Vitality determines how much damage a character can take before they die. When a character takes damage you lower both your current Health and Vitality, however when you receive healing of any kind only Health can be restored and only up to your Health maximum. Vitality can only be restored by resting. Should a characters Health be reduced to 0, then the character will be knocked unconscious and suffer 1 injury at the location struck. Should a characters Vitality be reduced to 0, then that character is dead.
 
# **Injuries**  
An injury can occur whenever a character suffers various effects. An injury on the character confers a penalty depending on the location hit.
- **Arm:** 
	You drop any object held in that hand, and the arm is broken or otherwise unable to move. You cannot perform any actions using that arm—by itself or alongside your other arm.
- **Leg:** 
	You immediately fall prone as your leg gives out under your weight. If one leg is broken, your movement is slowed, if both are broken you are prone until healed.
- **Torso:** 
	You begin bleeding heavily. At the end of each of your subsequent turns, you suffer a D8 physical damage, ignoring all your Damage Resistances. Each round you take damage while bleeding, increase the level of the Damage Dice for the bleed. Each time you go an entire round without taking damage, decrease the level of the Damage Dice. If the level of the Damage Dice is decreased so far that you take no damage, the bleeding stops.
- **Head:** 
	You are momentarily dazed and suffer a Lockout 75 . Concentration is also halved. These effects last until the injury has received medical attention.
 
# **Dying**  
When your character is reduced to 0 Health, they suffer an injury to the location struck, and then fall unconscious. If their Vitality is reduced to 0, they are dead. Any character with 3 or more injuries will remain unconscious until healed, furthermore any damage while unconscious this way will result in death. Should the entire party fall unconscious but not die out right, then the GM should set up a new scenario based on how they were defeated.
 
# **Healing**  
When you’ve taken damage, there are a number of ways you can recover. In the midst of combat, healing potions, magic and first aid are the only ways to heal, but outside of combat you have several options. The Medicine and Resistance skills are the most useful skills for healing.
 
# **Stabilizing The Unconscious**  
Using the First Aid action, you can attempt to stabilize an unconscious character. This requires an Medicine test with a difficulty equal to the number of injuries the patient has. Passing the test restores the character to 1 Health and they are no longer unconscious. If necrotic damage has reduced their maximum health points to 0, they are in a coma and cannot be stabilized until their maximum health points are above 0, by healing the necrotic damage.
 
# **Treating an Injury**  
You can attempt an Medicine test to treat an injury. Passing the test allows a patient to ignore the penalties of their injury. An injury treated using First Aid is not fully healed: it’s merely been patched up so that it no longer imposes a penalty. Whenever a character suffers any damage to a location which has a treated injury, make a Resistance test with difficulty equal to the number of injuries you have. If you fail, then the damage has re-opened that wound and the character is injured again. Completely recovering from an injury takes time.
 
# **Rest**  
When you sleep, you regain your max Health, also any injuries healed with First Aid is healed. If you have any injuries not treated with First Aid, make an Resistance test with a difficulty equal to the number of untreated injuries. If you succeed then you may heal one injury. In order to restore Vitality you need to either eat food or go a week without suffering damage. Eating food will restore both Health and Vitality but not by a lot, it can be the little extra you need in order to reach a town or find a safe place. If you stay safe and don't suffer any damage for a week then your Vitality is restored to full.
 
# **Medical Attention**  
Characters may require long-term medical care if they’ve been injured, poisoned, or are suffering from a disease. A single character can provide medical attention for a number of patients equal to their Medicine rating. For injuries, each day of rest and medical attention a patient receives allows you to assist their Resistance test at the end of the day to heal their injuries, using your own Medicine target number. For poisons and diseases, you may assist the patient’s Resistance tests to help them recover. If the patient spent the entire day resting, they reduce the difficulty of this test by 1 allowing them to recover more quickly.
