In combat, knowing where everyone is can be vital. The Roleplaying Game uses a system of hexagonal grids to divide up combat environments and measure movement and range quickly and easily. Each combat encounter happens in a single location. This may be a ruined building, a city street, an area of wilderness, the floor of a mansion, or any other scenario the GM might set up.
 
# **Mapping your Environment**  
Hexes aren’t fixed in size. Instead, they are as large or small as they need to be to describe the terrain, and they can be varied to accommodate the spaces in the encounter’s environment. Under most circumstances areas should be easy to describe—a few seconds to narrate the spaces and their relative positions, or to sketch out a rough map, with counters representing characters. Of course, this doesn’t prevent your GM from coming up with elaborate environments if they want to spend more time mapping out their encounters.

# **Environmental Conditions**  
Individual zones can have environmental conditions, like cover, difficult terrain, and hazards. Similarly, the environment as a whole may have some conditions that affect combat, like darkness or fog.
 
# **Cover**  
You can use your environment to your advantage in combat by standing behind it. When you do you'll get an increase to your damage threshold. Depending on what you are standing behind you can get different DT increases. Certain covers makes certain Hit Locations unavailable, meaning if you are standing behind a stone wall that covers your legs, if your opponent shoots at you and hits your legs after rolling for Hit Location, then that attack hit the stone wall and not you.
## **Example Covers**

| **Cover Type**                            | **DT Value** | **DT Origin** |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------- |
| Foliage, Wood                             | -            | Physical      |
| Rubble, Ruined Brick Walls, Metal Fencing | -            | Physical      |
| Concrete Walls, Steel Barricades          | -            | Physical      |
| Arcane Wards                              | -            | Magical       |
| Font of Life                              | -            | Necrotic      |
   
# **Difficult Terrain**  
Difficult terrain describes any ground that requires more effort to cross, either because it hinders you or because you need to be careful where you step. These terrains usually require you to spend more Action Points in order to move through them. Some terrain may also have additional effects, such as a gas cloud that harms you without necessarily increasing the movement cost. When difficult terrain is present, the GM should declare what it is and what extra cost or consequence it carries. However, this remains at the GM's discretion, and some dangers may be left unstated to preserve immersion.
 
## **Difficult Terrain Examples**

| **Terrain**                      | **AP Cost** |
| -------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Thick mud, loose sand, up stairs | 1           |
| Swamp, unstable rubble           | 2           |
| Steep slope, fast-flowing water  | 3           |

# **Obstacles**  
Obstacles may be barriers you need to climb up or over, or they might be gaps that you need to jump past. These obstacles require a skill test to get past and might include a higher Action Point cost than normal movement. If you need to get past an obstacle during combat and fail the associated test then your movement will end at the obstacle. Some obstacles might also cause failure to be harmful, such as failing to jump over a chasm. In such an event, not only is your movement ended, but you'll also suffer fall damage or a similar setback.
 
## **Obstacle Examples**

| **Obstacle**                                      | **Difficulty** |
| ------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Short jump, where failure is harmless             | 1              |
| Long jump, where failure might cause harm         | 2              |
| Tall climb, where failure might cause severe harm | 3              |

# **Environment Conditions**  
Some conditions affect multiple areas, or even the whole environment. Weather conditions and lighting are the most common examples of this.
## **Environmental Conditions Examples**

| **Condition**          | **Effect**                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Poor Lighting/Darkness | Tests that rely on vision become harder, and ranged attacks suffer an Accuracy penalty of +1, +2, or +3 depending on the light level |
| Mist and Fog           | Tests that rely on vision become harder, and ranged attacks suffer an Accuracy penalty when the target is at Medium range or further |
| Rain                   | Everyone standing in rain gains resistance to fire damage and ignore the Fire Effect.                                       |
| Slippery ground        | Everyone trying to move must make a associated test to not be Knocked Down.                                                 |

# **Hazards**  
Hazards are parts of the environment that inflict damage to creatures caught in them. A hazard may be present in a area of effect, or it may be spread among multiple areas. Hazards may be a persistent threat, or they might only occur if something specific happens to trigger them.
# **Dangerous Objects**  
An environment may contain objects that inflict damage or cause other effects based on the actions of those present - mines and other traps are common examples - as many folk in the world use traps and even animated minions to defend what is theirs. A dangerous object is triggered by a specific action occurring nearby. This might be a complication, or occur when a character enters a specific area. When triggered, a trap or dangerous object usually makes an attack against the relevant defenses of those caught by it. Magic might allow characters to set up traps like this quickly and easily.
