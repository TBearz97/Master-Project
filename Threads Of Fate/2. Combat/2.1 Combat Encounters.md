
Combat encounters involve characters attacking their opponents and defending themselves and their allies, and are more structured than freeform play. Instead of freely taking actions, the encounter is broken down into rounds and phases. At the start of the Movement Phase and the Action Phase, each creature in the scene replenishes their Action Points in full. Each round starts with the movement phase, each creature in the scene can spend any number of their Action Points to move where they desire, each hex costs 1 Action Point, unless there is difficult terrain or reaching a certain location requires a test, in that case the cost of movement may increase. After that there is the Action Phase, each creature in the scene determines what actions they would like to take, then when each creature has locked in their turn the GM narrates the combat scenario going through each action taken in order of their Speed value. After the Action Phase has been concluded, the Movement Phase begins again, this loop continues until the combat has come to a natural conclusion.

# **The Phases of Combat**

## **Combat Start**
When combat initiates the GM opens an encounter window and adds the characters to the map and any visible or invisible creatures that is in the area. Then the combat progresses through the phases: Movement Phase -> Action Phase -> Narrative Phase. These phases are repeated until either side can't or won't fight anymore. 

## **Movement Phase**  
Each round begins with a movement phase, where each creature involved acts in turn order based on their speed value. On each players turn they decide if and where they want to move, up to a number up hexes or grids equal to their Action Points available. Should the desired destination require you to make a test to reach, for example jumping a gap or climbing a wall, the test will cost extra Action Points equal to the difficulty of the test which is determined by the GM. Certain difficult terrain might also affect movement, but it might not cost extra Action Points, difficult terrain is explained further in the Environment section of this book. Further on certain Actions or Effects might tell you that your movement is slowed, when this happens any Action Points you would need to spend in the Movement Phase is doubled. Should your path be blocked by another creature you will immediately begin a struggle against each other's Athletics Skill, if you win you place the blocking creature in an adjacent hex, should you lose your movement is ended. Upon completing every creatures movement the Movement Phase is ended and we proceed to the Action Phase.
 
## **Action Phase**  
You can perform as many actions as your Action Points allow you to, then when every creature has chosen their actions for this turn we move into the Narrative Phase. Should you chose to do two Actions with the same Speed then those Actions will be performed after each other and cannot be broken up.
 
## **Narrative Phase**  
In the Narrative Phase the GM will narrate each Action after each other which is ordered by the creatures Tempo values. During this phase the GM may call on you to roll to Hit, for damage, for a test or otherwise require your input. Also certain Actions are not available in the Action Phase because they are reactive Actions, that means that they require certain conditions in order to be used. Those conditions usually happens in the Narrative Phase and it's the person controlling the creatures responsibility to realize when those conditions are met. When they are met they simply inform the GM, the GM will then stop narrating until you have resolved your reactive Action, then he will proceed narrating through the remaining Actions of the round. Important to note that even though the reactive Actions are not decided in the Action Phase, they will still require Action Points to be used, meaning that you need to save Action Points from the Action Phase to be used in the Narrative Phase if you want to use your reactive Actions. Inspiration Points generated in the Narrative Phase may exceed the maximum until the Narrative Phase is concluded. When the Narrative Phase is concluded, any excess Inspiration Points are lost and return to the Movement Phase unless the combat is concluded.

# Time, Speed, Swiftness and Slow
Every Action has a Speed value between 1-100, this determines when an action takes place in the narrative phase starting at Time 1 ending at Time 100. 

Each creature has a Swiftness Modifier between 0 and 22, which is used to reduce the Speed value of their action making them attack earlier. The Speed value can never be reduced below 1.

Certain effects have a Slow value, this is used to increase the Speed value of your attacks making them happen later. Should a Slow force planned attacks to happen after Time 100, then they are removed and will not happen.

Should two creatures do an Action at same Time value, then the prioritization is as follows: Players are before NPCs, then check level, if there are still conflicts then consult the GM. 

# **Hit Locations**
A target may have different armor values on different parts of their body, and in the heat of combat attacking strategically every time is a difficult thing to do. Therefore after an direct hit lands on a target you must roll a D20 to determine where on the target your attack lands. 
## **Choose Hit Location**
In some situations it might me desired to only hit certain locations or perhaps line up a extra powerful attack to hit a certain location. If you want to forgo the randomness of the "Hit Location Dice", then before you make your attack roll, choose a location you want the attack to hit and suffer an extra 3 difficulty for that attack.
## **Typical Hit Location Dice Result**

| **D20 Roll** | **Hit Location** |
| ------------ | ---------------- |
| 1            | Head             |
| 2-8          | Torso            |
| 9-11         | Left Arm         |
| 12-14        | Right Arm        |
| 15-17        | Left Leg         |
| 18-20        | Right Leg        |
## **Other Hit Locations**  
Some creatures may have other body parts—wings, claws, and tails—that do not appear on the hit location table. These locations will be listed in the creature’s statistics, replacing the Hit Locations table with its own. Also some Attacks might only have certain Locations as valid targets, these also use custom Hit Location Dice Tables.
# **Making an Attack**
A large part of combat revolves around attacking your opponent until either side can't or won't fight back. This is done by making attacks as a [[1.2 Skill Tests |skill test]] with the [[1.2 Skill Tests#**Difficulty**|difficulty]] being their appropriate defenses. Attacks can be broken down into two types, direct and area of effect, and these are handled differently.

## **Direct Attack**
1. Choose Weapon or spell:
	First thing you do when making a direct attack is to choose the weapon or spell you are using. 
2. Choose Hit Location (Optional):
	Choose whether you want to choose a location the attack hits or if you roll the hit location dice after. 
3. Make a skill test:
	Then you make a [[1.2 Skill Tests |skill test]] related to the weapon or spell, while taking the defense of the enemy as a [[1.2 Skill Tests#**Difficulty**|difficulty]]. (What defense they use is listed in the information of your weapon or spell.)
4. Calculate Damage:
	If the test resulted in a hit, you roll the hit location dice unless you chose a specific location, followed by rolling your damage dice. 
5. Spend [[1.3 Inspiration Points & Luck#**Combat **|IP]]:
	If you have any available you can spend IP to make your attack more lethal.

## **Area of Effect**

1. Choose Weapon or spell:
	First thing you do when making an area of effect attack is to choose the weapon or spell you are using. 
2. Choose Location:
	Choose where you want the attack to be landed, some spells and weapons require a target in their center.
3. Make a skill test:
	You make one [[1.2 Skill Tests |skill test]] related to the weapon or spell, while each enemy adds their defenses to the [[1.2 Skill Tests#**Difficulty**|difficulty]], resulting in some getting hit and some avoiding the damage. (What defense they use is listed in the information of your weapon or spell.)
4. Calculate Damage:
	If the test resulted in a hit follow it up by rolling your damage dice. 
5. Spend [[1.3 Inspiration Points & Luck#**Combat **|IP]]:
	If you have any available you can spend IP to make your attack more lethal.

# **Actions in Combat:**

- **Steady Aim (1 AP):** 
	If you are using a [[5.3 Ranged Weapons|Range Weapon]] re-roll 1d20 on the first attack roll you make this turn. This also skips your movement next round. 
	
- **Command an NPC (3 AP):** 
	If you have an allied NPC under your command, choose a target or task for them to achieve to the best of their ability. They also add your command skill to the first test they attempt.
	
- **Draw Item (2 AP):** 
	Draw one item carried on your person or pick up an object or item within your reach. You may put an item away as part of this action.
	
- **Focus (2 AP):** 
	Make a Discipline test, on a success you gain Mana equal to your Discipline Rank (Minimum of 1). After making this test you can spend 2 IP for 1 additional Mana. 
	
- **Interact (1 AP):** 
	Interact with your equipment or environment in a simple way, like opening a door, or pushing a button.
	
- **Hide (2 AP):** 
	If no enemies are within 5 hexes, you can attempt a Stealth test to hide. If an enemy is aware of your presence and not otherwise occupied, your Stealth test gains Difficulty 2 for each such enemy. When attempting to hide, treat the test like an area of effect attack: each aware enemy uses their own Investigation skill to increase the difficulty of your Stealth test. You are successfully hidden from any creature whose Investigation you overcome. If no enemies are close when you attempt to hide, they must instead roll an Investigation test against your Stealth to detect you. While hidden, your movement is slowed, and you cannot hide and reveal yourself intentionally in the same round.
	
- **Use Consumable (1 AP):** 
	Use a consumable that you are holding, targeting yourself or an adjacent willing character. If you’re not holding it, then you need to draw it first. 
	
- **Clear (2 AP):** 
	The character can attempt a skill test to clear an unwanted status effect or problem that afflicts the character, an animal the character is handling, or a carried piece of equipment. The test has a Difficulty of 2. The skill used varies based on the condition affecting the character. Tending to an animal companion’s conditions uses the Animal Handling skill.
    
- **Assist (2 AP):** 
	You [[1.2 Skill Tests#**Assistance**|assist]] an ally with their next test. If the ally you are assisting is under your command you perform a command test instead using that result, if the ally is an animal companion, use Animal Handling. 
	
- **Basic Weapon Attack (3 AP):** 
	Make a [[5.2 Melee Weapons|melee]] or [[5.3 Ranged Weapons|ranged]] attack. Your weapon might have more advanced attacks with different AP costs, see your weapon description for more information. 
	
- **Defend (4 AP):** 
	You focus on protecting yourself. Make a test using one of your four defenses. You choose the difficulty of this test, if you succeed, add the chosen difficulty to your chosen defense until the end of the next round.
	
- **Exploit (3 AP):** 
	The character takes additional time and concentration readying the next attack, seeking to find vulnerabilities in a single target’s defenses. The player nominates a target the character is able to perceive, then attempt an Investigation test with Difficulty 3 (modified for Investigation tests by distance, lighting, etc.). If this succeeds, the character’s next attack either ignores a chosen defense stat or a chosen DT . If you are damaged before you can make this attack, then the bonus is lost. 
	
- **First Aid (2 AP):** 
	You try to quickly patch the wounds of yourself or an ally. Make an Medicine test, with a difficulty equal to the number of injuries the patient has, and increase the difficulty by 1 if you are trying to perform first aid on yourself. If you succeed, you can either:
    - Heal HP equal to your Medicine Rank
    - [[2.3 Injury and Healing#**Treating an Injury**|Treat one injury]] the patient is suffering from
    - [[2.3 Injury and Healing#**Stabilizing The Unconscious**|Stabilize]] a dying patient 
    
- **Grab (2 AP):** 
	If you have a free hand you can Grab an opponent within reach. Attempt to [[2.2 Damage, Effects and Qualities#**Conditions**|grapple]] a target.
	
- **Inspire (3 AP):** 
	Make a Speech test with Difficulty 0. Generate IP based on your Charisma plus any additional you may gain from good rolls. Any IP generated can be distributed among yourself and your allies. 
	
- **Pass (0 AP):** 
	You choose not to do anything, saving up to 2 Action Points to be used your next turn.
	
- **Spell:** 
	Cast a spell, as described in the [[6. Magic/Magic Explained|Magic]] section. Each spell varies in how much Action Points they cost. 

- **Test:** 
	Perform a skill test for an action not covered by the other actions, with the gamemaster’s permission.
       
# **Minor Rules**

- **Prone:**
	You may be knocked prone by attacks, hazards, or complications. You may also drop prone willingly, at the end of any movement action you perform. Being prone has the following effects:
	- When you’re prone, you crawl. Your movement is slowed.
	- While you’re prone, enemies at range have a difficulty of 3 for any attacks against you.
	- While you’re prone, enemies adjacent to you reduce the difficulty by 3 for attacks.

- **Slow Movement:**
	Several things can slow your movement. While your movement is slowed you follow the following rules. ^0b3c9e
	- Movement Phase: The cost for each hex and grid is doubled.
	- Action Phase: All Actions while slowed are delayed by 15.
 
- **Sneak Attacks:**  
	If you successfully attack an enemy that is unaware of your presence, your damage dice are treated as having rolled their maximum value, resulting in a critical hit. You then roll the additional dice for the critical damage.
 
- **Hiding:**  
	If no enemies are within 5 hexes, you can attempt a Stealth test to hide. If an enemy is aware of your presence and not otherwise occupied, your Stealth test gains Difficulty 2 for each such enemy. When attempting to hide, treat the test like an area of effect attack: each aware enemy uses their own Investigation skill to increase the difficulty of your Stealth test. You are successfully hidden from any creature whose Investigation you overcome. If no enemies are close when you attempt to hide, they must instead roll an Investigation test against your Stealth to detect you. While hidden, your movement is slowed, and you cannot hide and reveal yourself intentionally in the same round.
