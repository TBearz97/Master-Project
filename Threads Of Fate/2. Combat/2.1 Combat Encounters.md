
Combat encounters involve characters attacking their opponents and defending themselves and their allies, and are more structured than freeform play. Instead of freely taking actions, the encounter is broken down into rounds and phases. At the start of each round, every creature refreshes their Action Points in full. Each round then moves through the Movement Phase, the Action Phase, and finally the Narrative Phase. This loop continues until the combat has come to a natural conclusion.

# **The Phases of Combat**

## **Combat Start**
When combat initiates the GM opens an encounter window and adds the characters to the map and any visible or invisible creatures that is in the area. Then the combat progresses through the phases: Movement Phase -> Action Phase -> Narrative Phase. These phases are repeated until either side can't or won't fight anymore. 

## **Movement Phase**  
Each round begins with a movement phase. During this phase, every creature decides where they want to move and traces their movement one hex or grid at a time. Each step normally costs 1 Action Point, though terrain, obstacles, or special circumstances can increase that cost. If you are threatened in melee, the first hex or grid you move costs additional Action Points. This increased cost stacks for each threatening opponent.

If two or more creatures would move into the same hex or grid, a conflict occurs and movement stops there. Any unused Action Points are refunded. The increased cost for leaving a threatened position does not apply when movement ends because of a conflict. Certain Actions or Effects might also slow movement, increasing the cost of moving during this phase. Once movement has been resolved, the encounter proceeds to the Action Phase.
 
## **Action Phase**  
You can perform as many actions as your Action Points allow you to, then when every creature has chosen their actions for this turn we move into the Narrative Phase. Should you chose to do two Actions with the same Speed then those Actions will be performed after each other and cannot be broken up.
 
## **Narrative Phase**  
In the Narrative Phase the GM resolves the chosen Actions in order of their Speed. Speed is determined by the kind of Action being taken and, when relevant, the weapon being used. During this phase the GM may call on you to roll to hit, roll damage, or otherwise provide input needed to resolve what was already chosen in the Action Phase. When the Narrative Phase is concluded, a new round begins unless the combat has ended.

# **Speed and Slow**
Every Action has a Speed value, which determines when it is resolved during the Narrative Phase. Speed is based on the Action Type and, when relevant, the Weapon Type being used.

Some effects can increase the Speed of an Action, causing it to happen later than normal. If two Actions would happen at the same Speed, the GM decides the order.

# **Hit Locations**
A target may have different armor values on different parts of their body, and in the heat of combat attacking strategically every time is a difficult thing to do. Therefore after an direct hit lands on a target you must roll a D20 to determine where on the target your attack lands. 
## **Choose Hit Location**
In some situations it might me desired to only hit certain locations or perhaps line up a extra powerful attack to hit a certain location. If you want to forgo the randomness of the "Hit Location Dice", then before you make your attack roll, choose a location you want the attack to hit and suffer -3 Accuracy for that attack.
## **Typical Hit Location Dice Result**

| **D20 Roll** | **Hit Location** |
| ------------ | ---------------- |
| 1            | Head             |
| 2-8          | Torso            |
| 9-11         | Left Arm         |
| 12-14        | Right Arm        |
| 15-17        | Left Leg         |
| 18-20        | Right Leg        |
## **Other Hit Locations**  
Some creatures may have other body parts—wings, claws, and tails—that do not appear on the hit location table. These locations will be listed in the creature’s statistics, replacing the Hit Locations table with its own. Also some Attacks might only have certain Locations as valid targets, these also use custom Hit Location Dice Tables.
# **Making an Attack**
A large part of combat revolves around attacking your opponent until either side can't or won't fight back. Attacks are not resolved as normal skill tests. Instead, attacks use an attack roll where you roll a d20 and add your Accuracy, then compare the result against the target's relevant defense. Attacks can be broken down into two types, direct and area of effect, and these are handled differently.

## **Direct Attack**
1. Choose Weapon or spell:
	First thing you do when making a direct attack is to choose the weapon or spell you are using. 
2. Choose Hit Location (Optional):
	Choose whether you want to choose a location the attack hits or if you roll the hit location dice after. 
3. Make an attack roll:
	Roll a d20 and add your Accuracy, then compare the result against the target's relevant defense. What defense is used depends on the weapon, spell, or effect being used.
4. Calculate Damage:
	If the test resulted in a hit, you roll the hit location dice unless you chose a specific location, followed by rolling your damage dice. If enough damage is dealt to the struck location to cause [[2.3 Injury and Healing#**Trauma**|Trauma]], resolve that after damage.

## **Area of Effect**

1. Choose Weapon or spell:
	First thing you do when making an area of effect attack is to choose the weapon or spell you are using. 
2. Choose Location:
	Choose where you want the attack to be landed, some spells and weapons require a target in their center.
3. Make an attack roll:
	Make one attack roll for the effect. Then compare that same result against each affected target's relevant defense. Some creatures may be hit while others avoid the effect.
4. Calculate Damage:
	If the test resulted in a hit follow it up by rolling your damage dice. If enough damage is dealt to a struck location to cause [[2.3 Injury and Healing#**Trauma**|Trauma]], resolve that after damage.

# **Actions in Combat:**


The following are common Actions that most characters can perform in combat. Exact Action Point costs are decided elsewhere.

- **Draw Item:** 
	Draw one item carried on your person or pick up an object or item within your reach. You may put an item away as part of this action.
	
- **Focus:** 
	You take time to gather power instead of acting immediately. Focus is a slow Action and is primarily used to build Mana. The exact benefits of Focus are explained later in the book.
	
- **Interact:** 
	Interact with your equipment or environment in a simple way, like opening a door, or pushing a button.
	
- **Hide:** 
	If no enemies are within 5 hexes, you can attempt a Stealth test to hide. If an enemy is aware of your presence and not otherwise occupied, your Stealth test gains Difficulty 2 for each such enemy. When attempting to hide, treat the test like an area of effect attack: each aware enemy uses their own Investigation skill to increase the difficulty of your Stealth test. You are successfully hidden from any creature whose Investigation you overcome. If no enemies are close when you attempt to hide, they must instead roll an Investigation test against your Stealth to detect you. While hidden, your movement is slowed, and you cannot hide and reveal yourself intentionally in the same round.
	
- **Use Consumable:** 
	Use a consumable that you are holding, targeting yourself or an adjacent willing character. If you’re not holding it, then you need to draw it first. 
	
- **Clear:** 
	The character can attempt a skill test to clear an unwanted status effect or problem that afflicts the character, an animal the character is handling, or a carried piece of equipment. The test has a Difficulty of 2. The skill used varies based on the condition affecting the character. Tending to an animal companion’s conditions uses the Animal Handling skill.
    
- **Assist:** 
	You help an ally with their next test. If the ally you are assisting is under your command you perform a command test instead using that result, if the ally is an animal companion, use Animal Handling. 
	
- **Attack:** 
	Make an attack using an Action granted by your weapon, spell, or other ability. Different weapons and powers grant different offensive Actions.
	
- **Guard:** 
	You protect yourself or another target against direct attacks by improving their Deflection for a short time.

- **Bolster:** 
	You reinforce a chosen defense on yourself or another target for a short time.
	
- **First Aid:** 
	You try to quickly patch the wounds of yourself or an ally. Make an Medicine test, with a difficulty equal to the number of injuries the patient has, and increase the difficulty by 1 if you are trying to perform first aid on yourself. If you succeed, you can either:
    - Heal HP equal to your Medicine Rank
    - [[2.3 Injury and Healing#**Treating an Injury**|Treat one injury]] the patient is suffering from
    - [[2.3 Injury and Healing#**Stabilizing The Unconscious**|Stabilize]] a dying patient 
    
- **Grab:** 
	If you have a free hand you can Grab an opponent within reach. Attempt to [[2.2 Damage, Effects and Qualities#**Conditions**|grapple]] a target.
	
- **Pass:** 
	You choose not to do anything for now.
	
- **Spell:** 
	Cast a spell, as described in the [[6. Magic/Magic Explained|Magic]] section. Each spell varies in how much Action Points they cost. 

- **Test:** 
	Perform a skill test for an action not covered by the other actions, with the gamemaster’s permission.
       
# **Minor Rules**

- **Prone:**
	You may be knocked prone by attacks, hazards, or complications. You may also drop prone willingly, at the end of any movement action you perform. Being prone has the following effects:
	- When you’re prone, you crawl. Your movement is slowed.
	- While you’re prone, enemies at range have a difficulty of 3 for any attacks against you.
	- While you’re prone, enemies adjacent to you reduce the difficulty by 3 for attacks.

- **Slow Movement:**
	Several things can slow your movement. While your movement is slowed you follow the following rules. ^0b3c9e
	- Movement Phase: The cost for each hex and grid is doubled.
	- Action Phase: All Actions while slowed are delayed by 15.
 
- **Sneak Attacks:**  
	If you successfully attack an enemy that is unaware of your presence, your damage dice are treated as having rolled their maximum value, resulting in a critical hit. You then roll the additional dice for the critical damage.
 
- **Hiding:**

When attempting to hide, treat the test like an area of effect attack: each aware enemy uses their own Investigation skill to increase the difficulty of your Stealth test. You are successfully hidden from any creature whose Investigation you overcome. If no enemies are close when you attempt to hide, they must instead roll an Investigation test against your Stealth to detect you. While hidden, your movement is slowed, and you cannot hide and reveal yourself intentionally in the same round.
