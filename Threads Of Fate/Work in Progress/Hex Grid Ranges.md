RANGE  
Melee weapons are only effective against targets in an adjacent hex, but ranged and thrown weapons can be used to attack any target you can see, but they vary in effectiveness depending on how far away the target is from you. Ranged weapons have an ideal range noted in their profile (just listed as Range), which is based on the relative distance between the attacker and the target, using the zones of the combat environment. A weapon’s ideal range is one of the following:

- Reach (R): The weapon is most effective against targets in an adjacent hex.
- Close (C): The weapon is most effective against targets within 2-4 hexes.
- Medium (M): The weapon is most effective against targets within 5-7 hexes.
- Long (L): The weapon is most effective against targets within 8-10.
- Extreme (X): The weapon is most effective against targets 11-13.

The difficulty of a ranged attack increases by one for each range band outside of the weapon’s ideal range, whether closer or further away—a long bow is deadly at Long ranges, but awkward and unwieldy in close quarters, while a pistol is great at Close range but less useful at longer distances.

In combat, knowing where everyone is can be vital. The Roleplaying Game uses a system of hexagonal grids to divide up combat environments and measure movement and range quickly and easily. Each combat encounter happens in a single location. This may be a ruined building, a city street, an area of wilderness, or the floor of a mansion.
 
MAPPING YOUR ENVIRONMENT  
Hexes aren’t fixed in size. Instead, they are as large or small as they need to be to describe the terrain, and they can be varied to accommodate the spaces in the encounter’s environment. Under most circumstances areas should be easy to describe—a few seconds to narrate the spaces and their relative positions, or to sketch out a rough map, with counters representing characters. Of course, this doesn’t prevent your GM from coming up with elaborate environments if they want to spend more time mapping out their encounters.
 
DISTANCE AND RANGES  
Movement and ranged attacks use descriptive terms to measure their distance in combat, relative to the objects’ placement. Range is measured in the following five categories:

- Reach is the adjacent hex, when an object or character is within arm’s length of your character. You can interact with objects and make melee attacks within your reach. Being in reach of an enemy is disruptive to ranged attacks and tests, adding +2 to the difficulty of any test that isn’t a melee attack.
- Close range represents anything within 2-4 hexes.
- Medium range is anything within 5-7 hexes.
- Long range represents anything within 8-10 hexes.
- Extreme range represents anything within 11-13 hexes.
 
AREAS OF EFFECT  
Areas of effect can take different shapes and sizes. In the description of spells and effects is listed the shape and the scale. Shapes take one of the following forms:

- Circles are measured by counting the radius with its center as 1 up to the scale times 3.
- Lines are measured by choosing an origin then drawing a line on the hex grid with length equal 3 times the scale.

The scale of the AOE is listed with a number usually ranging from 1-4. This is multipled with 3 and combined with a shape to bring the spell or effect into the game.
 
ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS  
Individual zones can have environmental conditions, like cover, difficult terrain, and hazards. Similarly, the environment as a whole may have some conditions that affect combat, like darkness or fog.
 
COVER  
Cover provides additional Damage Resistance against physical and energy-based attacks. Objects in the environment will provide a certain amount of DR, as a number of Combat Dice (CD), depending on what they’re made from and how resilient they are. When you are attacked by a physical or energy-based attack while behind cover, roll the number of CD listed in the Cover Values table and add that result to your DT for that attack. To benefit from cover, it needs to obscure the location hit, whether that’s from a melee or ranged attack. You can’t be targeted by an attack if the cover obscures your character’s entire body, as you can only be attacked by something that can see you.

DIFFICULT TERRAIN AND OBSTACLES  
Difficult terrain describes any ground that requires more effort to cross, either because it hinders you or because you need to be careful where you step. Obstacles may be barriers you need to climb up or over, or they might be gaps that you need to jump past. When you attempt to move from an area of difficult terrain, or cross an obstacle, you must spend one or more extra Inspiration Points to do so, depending on how difficult the terrain or obstacle is. If you do not have sufficient Inspiration Points available, then you need to find some way to generate them.

ENVIRONMENT CONDITIONS  
Some conditions affect multiple areas, or even the whole environment. Weather conditions and lighting are the most common examples of this.

|---|---|

HAZARDS  
Hazards are parts of the environment that inflict damage to creatures caught in them. A hazard may be present in a area of effect, or it may be spread among multiple areas. Hazards may be a persistent threat, or they might only occur if something specific happens to trigger them.
 
DANGEROUS OBJECTS  
An environment may contain objects that inflict damage or cause other effects based on the actions of those present—mines and other traps are common examples—as many folk in the world use traps and even animated minions to defend what’s theirs. A dangerous object is triggered by a specific action occurring nearby. This might be a complication, or occur when a character enters a specific area. Characters must attempt a test to avoid the damage (AGI + Acrobatics or PER + Investigation to either dodge the trap or to spot the trigger just in time). Magic allow characters to set up traps like this quickly and easily.
